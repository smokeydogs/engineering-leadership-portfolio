# From quality leadership to digital product delivery

**Organization:** Houghton Mifflin Harcourt  
**Period:** March 2001–May 2015  
**Domain:** K–12 digital education, assessment, and mobile learning

## Career progression

- **Quality Assurance:** QA Analyst through Lead, Manager, and Director of Quality Assurance, 2001–2010
- **Director of Application Development:** October 2010–April 2014
- **Director of Program Management:** April 2014–May 2015

## Context

HMH was expanding from traditional educational publishing into online and mobile learning, digital assessment, interactive content, and data-informed instruction. Public HMH material from the period describes iPad-based curriculum, integrated instruction and assessment, offline access, student-response workflows, and digital submission capabilities.

## Leadership challenge

Delivering large digital education programs required coordinated growth across software development, quality engineering, program management, vendors, and distributed delivery locations—all while meeting school-year and market commitments.

## My contribution

### Quality leadership

- Earned successive promotions from QA Analyst to Director of Quality Assurance, reaching the director role in 2005.
- Grew QA from 3 analysts to more than 40 professionals across locations.
- Reduced manual testing time by more than 56% through automation and reusable regression suites.

### Application development

- Scaled development and quality from fewer than 5 to more than 95 team members across multiple locations.
- Achieved average sprint completion of 90%.
- Launched an online and mobile education product in six months, generating $30 million in annual revenue.
- Restructured global delivery and consolidated a satellite office of more than 40 resources, saving $2 million annually.
- Standardized vendor rates, saving an additional $400,000 annually in contingent labor.

### Program management

- Built and led the online testing division’s program-management office across 95 resources and 15 projects.
- Kept project overruns below 5% while delivering 90% of requested roadmap features.
- Standardized forecasting, estimation, work intake, and portfolio reporting.

## Leadership interpretation

This progression shaped my view that engineering, quality, and program leadership are parts of one delivery system. Scaling teams without common planning, feedback, and accountability mechanisms creates capacity without predictable outcomes. Connecting those disciplines enabled both product delivery and operating leverage.
